# The attack loop

## Attack loop (`ui/attack.py`, `parsers/scout.py`, `parsers/glyphs.py`)

攻擊 → 尋找對戰目標 → 攻擊 → scout → deploy or skip → 回營, driven by `AttackRunner`. **Nothing in the loop asks Gemini what is on screen.** The scout screen expires after 30 seconds and then forces the battle, so `read_scout` reads the loot against digit templates in about 0.15 s. It answers None (正在搜尋對手, or a panel it cannot read), a view with `can_skip=True`, or one with `can_skip=False` (read off the 下一個 button's orange).

**Reading the loot panel.**

- **`read_scout`'s None is overloaded**, and searching and unreadable are opposite instructions. A search is sat out for `SEARCH_PATIENCE`, never left early: the game goes on searching alone and its countdown then starts a battle with nothing deployed. The end of a battle is `_battle_ended`, which reads the green 回營 button, never a None.
- A new opponent fades in, and a frame caught mid-fade reads half-drawn digits and an absent 下一個 — which means "forced" and commits the army. `_scout` refuses a frame whose panel peak (`panel_peak`) is under `PANEL_DRAWN_BRIGHTNESS`. The check lives in `_scout` rather than `read_scout`, because the battle poll must still read popup-dimmed panels.
- **The double-loot event paints its own panel** (yellow digits, lower rows, a cupcake after each number), read by a second pass (`DOUBLE_ROW_BOUNDS`, `_double_ink`) only when the ordinary one fails, so an ordinary panel reads exactly as before.
- **Skipping is bounded both ways, by the user's rule (2026-11-21)**: `UNREADABLE_OPPONENTS` unreadable opponents in a row across the series (`_unread_row`, so a search window running out does not reset it) and the next is attacked blind; `POOR_SKIPS` in a row under the thresholds within one search (`_poor_row`) and the next is attacked anyway. Each sets `AttackReport.waived`, which the round line and `attack`'s report print, since each means the reader or the thresholds need looking at.
- The storage bars (`read_stock`) are the home village test of every `GameRunner` loop (`_home`), so a failure there takes `collect` and `donate` down; the attack loop tests the village with `current_world`.

**Around the battle.**

- `army_strength` reads the camp off 我的軍隊 before the search fee is charged. Training is instant, so an army under `MIN_ARMY_RATIO` (0.5) cannot fill and ends the whole series (`army_short`) instead of resting.
- `--until-idle` reads the builder and laboratory counters in the bottom right (`plate_count`), or only the one it names, off the same village frame `_stood_down` reads the storages from, ahead of them, and ends the series `builder_free` or `lab_free` once either reads an idle count above 0. Only the idle count decides, never the total, because events add a builder or a research slot (drawn with a goblin face) and take it away again. Any idle slot counts, including one idle from the first round, because a farming loop is restarted around every loan and a remembered starting count would swallow whatever freed up in the gap; a counter that will not read plays the round. It is read once, so a caller confirms it with `status` (the export's running jobs against the counter) before telling anyone. Without the flag nothing reads them, and no skill passes it since `takeover` waits on `status`'s `done_at` instead (#583).
- **A series says how it ended**, which its last round cannot: `attack` answers an `AttackRun` whose required `ending` (`SeriesEnding`) is set on every way out of the round loop, a stop pending when `--repeat` runs out included, and the journal's `end` line carries it as its `outcome`.
- **A series says what it brought home.** Each `AttackReport` carries what the runner read around its round (`holding` before the search, `capacity`, the builder base's `cart` and the battles since its last trip, `commands._seen`), so `commands.farmed` works the loot, the battles no two readings bracket, the cart and the elixir still waiting in it out of the series alone, and `attack` prints that with its ending (`attack_text`) and the rounds behind `--json`. The journal's `round` line keeps its own copies of those three, which is what `ai_coc farmed` (`commands.farmed_runs`) reads to sum a session's runs, each as `farmed` sums a series, lines from before the reports carried them included.
- The tactic goes in as soon as the plan is back, inside the scout countdown: a drop there starts the battle, and the boundary is already drawn. Only `probe` and `bounds` still wait the countdown out (`_wait_for_battle`).
- Every battle opens with a pinch out (`_settle_zoom`), because the game reports no zoom level. The boundary's height before the pinch warns on a clipped, zoomed-in home village. The builder base is smaller at every zoom, so the warning is the home village's alone, and a zoomed-in builder base goes undetected: no recorded frame has one to draw a floor from.
- **The result screen** (`RETURN_HOME_GREEN`, `_leave_result`). `_leave_result` re-reads before pressing `back`, and `_open_attack_menu` hands any frame that is no village to `uncovered`, which clears a popup left over the result screen and still never presses at a battle. The report the game opens on after a raid (首領，歡迎回來！, `welcome_back`) puts its one 確定 where 回營 sits, so `battle_over` refuses it, and `uncovered` and `_settle_game` both press `back` at it. The 魔法護盾 sheet the old shield plate opened does the same with its 繼續, and `shield_sheet` keeps it out of `battle_over` the same way. So does the 勝利之星 reward sheet (`star_bonus`), raised a few times a day when the star bonus is won, which `_settle_game` presses `back` at as well, and so does the notice an event's end raises (`event_ended`), which stands at more than one height and gets a tap on the map outside its sheet instead (`EVENT_NOTICE_CLOSE`), the one press seen to close it; `back` at it has not been tried. A reward chest the game raises over the whole screen after some battles (`reward_chest`) ignores `back`, so `uncovered`, `_settle_game` and `_home` tap it open and press 繼續 on each reward it shows (`open_chest`); that green button is pressed only after a chest was read, where `open_chest` opened the chest or found its reward already standing, and only inside `CHEST_CONTINUE_BOX`, which leaves 立即完成 and the home village's 確認 out; nothing checks that each look after the chest is still its reward, so a star bonus raised straight after one has its button pressed. The tools on a chest's strip are the event's (hammers on 2026-10-16, rolling pins on 2026-11-17), so the chest is read by its hammers or by the 跳過 its screen carries (`chest_skip`), and tapped until the screen changes (`screen_changed`) rather than until the tools run out. Known and open: a chest of another tool that somebody began tapping shows neither, and reads as no chest.

**The plan** (`_plan`, `AttackPlan`, `AttackStep`, `prompts/attack_plan.md`).

- **The default tactic and the prompt's tactic are the user's.** `flat.json`'s drop order is the one he dictated (#349), its spell times the ones he tuned (#362) and its drop spots the ones he drew (2026-11-17: the troops packed along the left-corner end of the top-left flank instead of the whole of it, the heroes in the middle of that stretch, the siege machine outside the left corner; the rage in rows parallel to that flank, stepping inward along the diamond's own axis, never along the screen's), and it is the default farming tactic; the prompt's choices below (the side with the heaviest defences, the siege machine after every troop and hero, rage behind the troops) are his too. Ask him before changing either; a variant for a comparison is saved as a file of its own and fed through `--plan`. What he reports about a tactic is a hypothesis to measure, not a conclusion.
- **Every battle keeps the frame its first drop follows** (`_keep_opening`, called from `_flank`): `frames/opening.png` in the run's own directory, `--debug` or not, one name for the whole run so a series holds only its newest battle's, with a log line giving the camera's shift. It is what `ai_coc draw --run` draws a plan and the pointing grid on, and the `attack-plans` skill owns how a tactic is discussed on such a picture; a discussion uses a frame of a recent battle or one played for it, never a fixed picture (the user, 2026-11-21). A frame that will not write costs a warning, not the battle.
- `tests/frames/battle_flat_reference.png` and `tests/frames/night_flat_reference.png` are two such frames, kept as fixtures for `tests/test_draw.py`: each is the screen right before the first drop with the whole card row showing. The home one was taken after the flank's camera drag of (0, 102); the builder base one had no drag, and was taken while the battle machine was upgrading, so its row is the six troop cards without the machine the plan sends first.
- **`planned_drops` is the runner's arithmetic a second time, for `ai_coc draw`**: it repeats how `_deploy`, `_act`, `_cast`, `_cast_some` and `_deploy_night` string the shared pieces together (`deploy_candidates`, `fitted_line`, `onto_line`, `spaced`, `single_spots`) instead of sharing that with them, so the loop itself stayed untouched. `tests/test_draw.py` plays the runner against it on the reference frames and fails when one moves without the other; a change to where a step's taps go changes both in the same PR.
- One Gemini call per battle, after the thresholds have passed, so a skipped opponent costs nothing. `--plan` plays a written plan with no call (a path, or the name of one shipped in `plans/`, `plans.NAMES`, loaded for the village the series reads), `--plan-out` writes whichever ran, and `plans.flat()` (`plans/flat.json`) is what a run with no key or a failed call gets.
- A plan is an ordered list of steps, played in order, and it carries every clock: where each hero goes (`hero`), when each ability fires (`ability`), where each spell goes and when. `act`, `who`, `at` and `seconds` have no defaults, because Gemini leaves out fields that have one. Nothing in `config.json` holds a clock.
- A `wait` counts from the previous move finishing, so nothing depends on how long the call or the boundary read took.
- `hero` steps are matched to the cards left to right (an upgrading hero's card disappears) and are skipped when the cards run out. The cards follow the order the player set the army in, which has differed between armies, so a written `--plan` cannot know which hero a card is: `flat.json` names only its first hero, and sends the rest as `unknown`; one naming `unknown` sends every hero still in hand. Which one-off card is the siege machine comes from the plan having a `siege` step, not from a health bar, since the game draws one over the siege machine too.
- The prompt asks for the side with the **heaviest** defences (troops take defences head on) and for rage on the heaviest defence group, where the army ends up. Moving rage onto measured motion (`_onto_army`, removed) landed the bottles seconds after the plan asked for them (`test_the_rage_goes_where_the_plan_drew_it`).
- The plan decides the order of the drops. The order the loop used to fix was siege machine, troops, then heroes straight behind. The siege machine now goes down after every troop and hero, in `flat.json` and in the prompt.
- One point per bottle.
- **Troops and spells can be split over several steps of one act**. Before the last step of its act, a troop step pours an even share of each card along the plan's one line and a spell step casts one bottle per point; the last step empties the cards (`_spent` carries what was taken). Only neighbouring spell steps are merged, so a split needs a `wait` between its halves. `_settle_drops` judges the troop row only once its last step has played, since a card holding a later share is holding on purpose.
- `planned_line` rejects a line whose midpoint is within `MIN_LINE_RADIUS` of the centre, since `push_out` cannot move it; the plan is kept and its own `deploy_from` preset flank is played instead (`deploy_candidates(plan)[0]`); no other flank is tried.

**Deployment.**

- The whole tactic goes down blind in bursts (`tap_many`), then `_settle_drops` reads a frame inside a pause of at least `CHECK_BUDGET` that follows a one-off drop (or once after the last step, when none did) and resends what the game refused (`_drop_singles`, `_spread_troops`). A refused drop costs nothing, because the card is not consumed. Later pauses check the battle is still on, since some card slots sit where the result screen draws 回營.
- **A drop is judged by the card, never by the screen.** 你無法在紅線區域內派遣部隊 and the other red banners share one colour and are not given reliably. `card_drained` reads the `xN` corner repainting, which happens on the first troop or bottle to leave, so whether a card is *empty* is `live_cards`; a hero landing is `field_units`, reading the green health bar, told from grass by how little blue it has (`HERO_BAR_MAX_BLUE`).
- The card row: `card_groups` is valid only on a full row, so `_deploy` reads it once, off the frame captured after the pinch and before anything is dropped. Known and not fixed: `BattleRow` keeps the x each card had when the row was read, so a card the game adds at the front mid-battle shifts every card after it, and `ability` and `_cast` then tap one slot off; `_dump_leftovers` is the one step written to survive it.
- Timing constants are measured, not cautious. ADB was never the cost (`tap_many` has the measurements).

**The map and the camera in battle.**

- `DEPLOY_BOUND` is the ground the game accepts a drop on, measured with `ai_coc bounds` rather than detected. `push_out` clamps to it, and every drop point goes through `clear_of_controls`, which keeps it inside `PLAYFIELD`, off the card row and off 放棄. `single_spots` drops a pushed point the game clamps straight back and tries the next spot.
- **The formation diamond** (`boundary_diamond`, the frame `ai_coc draw`'s pointing grid is cut from; the user judged it accurate on 2026-11-21, so its method stays). The red line runs along the map's own two directions, which on screen climb or fall 0.75 px for every px across (`MAP_SLOPE`, read off the stroke rather than assumed), so the diamond is fitted to the line's edges and never to corners picked by eye. Every stroke pixel on the playfield, the UI's own red blanked and any pixel with stroke red `DIAMOND_THIN` above or below it dropped as scenery, is binned by `y - 0.75x` and by `y + 0.75x`; a bin whose longest run reaches `DIAMOND_RUN` px through gaps of `DIAMOND_GAP` is an edge, and the outermost edge each way bounds the diamond. The corners are where those four lines cross, so a corner can land off the screen or under the card row as the line does; edges closer than `DIAMOND_SPAN` are no outline and the answer is None, and a corner with no stroke within `CORNER_REACH` is named `empty`, since the diamond is only as square as the base. It is for a frame taken before the first drop and answers None on a village or on a battle whose line has gone to pieces. No loop reads it; a caller that came to depend on it mid-battle would need frames of that measured first.
- **The map's edge is not read, and `draw` does not mark it** (the user, 2026-11-21, after the attempts below). The game draws no UI at it, only scenery, and three ways of finding it failed on the 12 battle frames of that day and the committed references: a fixed outline, because the battle opens with the map up to about 80 px apart sideways from one village to the next with no camera drag to account for it; the tile checker some sceneries paint, because the red line and its refused ground run past the checker (it is the ground buildings stand on, not where troops can go); and a colour step along the map's two directions outside the formation diamond, which locks onto dirt paths and tree lines. Nor does `draw` mark the ground a drop is taken on: the light-yellow tint it carried for one PR (#694) covered the wrong ground (the user, 2026-11-21), and he will say how that ground should be marked, so no tint goes back on without him. Filling the red line's inside from outside the stroke was tried for it and leaked into the village on 15 of 17 battle frames of that day, where the stroke over bright or teal ground fails `boundary`'s colour test and leaves gaps up to 100 px long.
- The game draws its own red stroke around the refused ground. `boundary_reach` reads it along one ray, and `fitted_line` bends the plan's line (or its `deploy_from` preset, when `planned_line` refused the line) onto it through three anchors. It is an opening guess, and `_spread_troops` pushing the flank out when a pass drains nothing is the backstop. `ai_coc probe` measures how good the guess is; `fitted_line`'s outlier check says what its first run found.
- `_settle_camera` centres the village before `_plan`. Known and not fixed: the frame it measures is still the scout screen, whose orange 下一個 button `village_box` masks only below y 600 (`UI_PANELS`), so the box read x 1500 against 1269 for the same village moments later and a run can spend two drags shoving a centred village 72 px. Moving the call onto a battle frame puts it after `_plan`, which would need a second coordinate frame. Also known and open (#65): `village_box` barely follows the camera at all, because its 2% trim lands on village-art speckle near the playfield edges, so `_settle_camera` cannot see a camera that really is off-centre and `_clear_flank` drags 86-102 px on every battle, whatever size the village is. Deployment works with that drag today, so `_clear_flank` and `FLANK_ROOM` stay as they are until #65 is settled. `_clear_flank` drags the village away from whichever edge the flank faces (`FLANK_ROOM`): up, off the card row, for a lower flank, and down for an upper one. `_pan` / `_panned` record every camera move that leaves the village off centre (not `_settle_camera`'s own, which ends centred), measured by `view_shift` along the drag, so the flanks and the plan keep pointing at the village. The loop never selects a card before a drag, since with one selected a swipe deploys along its path; that is also how a machine the game preselects at the start of a builder base second stage gets sent.

## Builder base (`AttackRunner.world`, `_run_night`, `_night_plan`)

The same runner plays both villages. An attack series takes its village from the first round that reads one, and ends `other_village` on two readings of the other village; it never sails.

- **Getting in**: 攻擊 → 開始進攻 → 立即尋找. No scout screen, no thresholds, no fee and no army check, since the army refills the moment a battle ends. The matchmaker waits for a live player (one search took five and a half minutes); `searching_opponent` reads its 取消, and a search past `SEARCH_PATIENCE` is cancelled and started again.
- **The card row** is the machine first and the troops after it; the troops are the cards with a count.
- **`live_cards` means the unit died there**, not that the card is empty. So `_spread_night` decides by whether anything has drained yet: a barren pass before any drain pushes the flank out, and one after stops the passes. It does answer "is the machine still alive" correctly, so a machine that died in stage one is not sent in stage two.
- The machine lands first, with `NightPlan.troops_after` as its head start. Its ability recharges all battle, so `_wait_out_night` taps every one-off card each `ABILITY_TAP` and captures every `ABILITY_POLL`. A machine that survived opens stage two preselected: `_send_selected` taps the field and checks `selected_cards`, since a damaged health bar does not read as one (`CARD_SELECTED_SPAN` lets `card_groups` see the wider selected card).
- A stage can end with no result screen; the card row being repainted marks it, not counting a card whose selection changed, since the ability taps move the selection and that repaints a corner too. A second stage is played when offered, never predicted. Known and not fixed: `_night_plan` runs on every stage and each overwrites the plan the round records, so the journal's `round` line keeps only the last stage's; under `--plan night_flat.json` both stages play the same plan anyway.
- **The storage limits** are checked before the search, which can take minutes. See **Storage limits** below.
- Known and open: `fitted_line` almost never fits here. Over the 2026-10-16/17 runs (153 rounds) it logged `No boundary on the ray … keeping the preset flank` 249 times and fitted twice, so night battles deploy on the plan's own unbent line (`night_flat.json`'s is the `top_left` preset) and lean on `_spread_night` pushing it out.

**The loot cart** (`collect_cart`, `loot_cart_open`, `loot_cart_ready`, `loot_cart_load`, `CartReport`). Gold lands in the storages; battle elixir goes into a 聖水車 beside the boat, and tapping it opens a sheet.

- The sheet is closed on every path.
- The cart is the builder base's third storage: defence rewards keep paying into it after the storages are full, so full storages end a run only when the cart is also past `stop_at` of its own ceiling (`_visit_cart`, `_cart_has_room`). A look that cannot say how full the cart is goes again at once, and after three the storages decide alone; so does a payout that filled the elixir storage to its ceiling, since what it had no room for is still in the cart. One schedule, the first round and every `CART_EVERY` battles, empties the cart while the storages have room and reads it once they do not.
- Judged on `read_builder_stock`, never `read_stock`.

## Storage limits

`AppConfig.stop_at` is one share for both villages, measured against ceilings read off the game (`storage_capacity`), so nothing typed in goes stale. Tapping a bar opens a 最大儲存量 tooltip; `AttackRunner._settle_ceilings` and `ScreenRunner.read_ceilings` read it once per run. The two villages draw it differently since game version 19.32.4: the builder base's is the translucent toggle, shut by a second tap on its bar, and the home village's an opaque pale panel that a tap on its own bar leaves open and a tap on the panel puts away (`TOOLTIP_DROP`). A tap can close a tooltip already open instead of opening its own, so a row that reads nothing is left alone and tried again; a failed read ends by shutting whichever tooltip `open_tooltip` finds left open, since one hangs over the + buttons `current_world` reads. The builder base's third row is the gems bar, whose `+` opens the shop, and is never tapped. **Every watched storage has to be full**, not one of them (`StorageCapacity.full`). A resource with no ceiling is left out and neither stops a run nor holds one open, which is what the builder base's missing dark elixir relies on. A read where any tapped row fails is thrown away whole. **A storage above its ceiling is real, not a misread**: resources that come with a purchase land past the ceiling (gold read 153% on 2026-10-12; the user: 「目前金幣超出上限是正常的 因為購買點數送的金幣 聖水 黑水 是會超過上限的 不用太在意」), and `full` counts it as full.
